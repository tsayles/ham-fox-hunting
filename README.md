# Ham Fox Hunting — Mike & Key ARC RDF Program

> **Hunt the Signal. Build. Practice. Compete.**

A tools and resources repository supporting the
[Mike & Key Amateur Radio Club Fox Hunting Program](https://mikeandkey.org/foxhunt.php)
— a structured, multi-phase Radio Direction Finding (RDF) initiative for
the Pacific Northwest amateur radio community.

---

## What is Fox Hunting?

Fox hunting (transmitter hunting / ARDF) is the sport of using radio
direction finding to locate a hidden transmitter — the "fox." Participants
use directional antennas and signal-strength techniques to triangulate the
transmitter's position. It builds practical RF skills, antenna theory,
navigation, and teamwork in the field.

The Mike & Key program covers three hunt formats:

| Format | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pedestrian (ARDF)** | On-foot hunts in parks; map, compass, and handheld RDF gear |
| **Mobile** | Vehicle-based hunts across the region using vehicle-mounted or handheld antennas |
| **Fixed Location** | Teams determine transmitter coordinates remotely — accessible to all skill and mobility levels |

## Program Structure

### Phase 1 — Build Season 🔧

Hands-on workshops at multiple regional locations:

- **2-meter directional antenna construction** (tape measure Yagi)
- **Step attenuator construction**
- Equipment calibration and field testing
- Beginner-friendly sessions — no prior RDF experience required

### Phase 2 — Practice Hunts 🗺️

Mentored, low-pressure training events:

- Park-based hunts with guided instruction
- Signal tracking and bearing technique
- Team formats and equipment checkout options
- Mentors paired with new participants

### Phase 3 — Hunt Events 🦊

Community events that bring people together around the sport:

- Multiple transmitters spread across varied terrain
- Emphasis on fun, camaraderie, and shared experience
- Social gatherings before and after each hunt
- Open to all licensees and experience levels — come as a team or solo

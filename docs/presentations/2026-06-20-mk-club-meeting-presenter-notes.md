### Slide 4 — The Concept
**[Layout C | ~3:30]**

The basic idea is that we hide a transmitter, which we call the fox, and
participants use radio direction finding to locate it. You can describe RDF as
taking signal bearings with a directional antenna and turning those bearings
into a search path. Emphasize that this is used around the world for sport,
training, and simple club fun, so it is both legitimate and accessible. Any
license class can join in, and complete beginners can learn the mechanics
quickly. Now let's look at the two main ways people do this in amateur radio.

---

### Slide 5 — Two Flavors of Fox Hunting
**[Layout C | ~5:00]**

Explain that on-foot ARDF usually means walking or running with a handheld
directional antenna, while a mobile T-hunt means taking bearings from a
vehicle and converging on the fox from the road network. The gear and pace
feel different, but the core skill is the same: read the signal, choose a
bearing, and make a good next move. Tell the audience this program covers both
because the club has members who will enjoy each style. That shared skill set
is why the equipment and practice opportunities later in the talk fit together
so naturally. Before I move on, let me show the signal behavior that makes
direction finding possible.

---

### Slide 6 — How Direction Finding Works
**[Layout E | ~6:30]**

Walk through the loop slowly: point the Yagi, rotate until the signal peaks,
note the bearing, move, and repeat. Explain that multiple bearings let you
triangulate the likely hide area, even if you never see the transmitter until
the end. Mention that a step attenuator becomes useful when you are close,
because the signal can otherwise overwhelm the receiver and make every
direction seem strong. This is a nice place to say that fox hunting mixes
radio technique with judgment about terrain and movement. Now let's freeze
that idea on a pattern graphic so the front lobe is easy to visualize.

---

### Slide 7 — Antenna Pattern
**[Layout D | ~8:00]**

Use this slide to reinforce that the antenna does not hear equally in all
directions, and that is exactly why it helps us hunt. Point out the strong
forward lobe and explain that the hunter is listening for the best peak, not
just any signal. If helpful, mention that side and rear response can create
ambiguity, which is why comparing several headings matters. This is the
technical picture behind the simple body motion people see in the field. Now
let's move from diagrams to a real club hunt scene.

---

### Slide 11 — The Hunter's Antenna — Tape Measure Yagi
**[Layout C | ~13:00]**

Describe the tape-measure Yagi as the sweet spot for club use because it is
cheap, durable, light, and easy to build in a group. A three-element 2-meter

Mention that it connects easily to a handheld with a BNC fitting, which keeps
the entry path simple for people using common ham gear. This is also a good
preview of Radio Camp, because building these antennas is the first hands-on

important near the fox.

---

### Slide 12 — The Close-In Tool — Step Attenuator
**[Layout C | ~14:00]**

Explain that the attenuator lives between the handheld and the antenna, and
its job is to make a very strong nearby signal readable again. Without it, the
S-meter can feel useless in the last few yards because every direction looks
loud and the hunter starts chasing noise instead of direction. This is one of
those simple tools that makes the learning experience much less frustrating
for beginners. Mention that it is also planned as a Radio Camp build project
if time allows. Now let's talk about the lowest-cost radio option people may
ask about.

---

### Slide 13 — Entry-Level Radio — Radtel RT-910B
**[Layout C | ~15:00]**

Present the RT-910B as a promising low-cost option, not as a fully endorsed
club standard. Say that the appeal is the roughly $30 price point, the large
S-meter display, and the TX lockout feature that could be useful for demos or
loaners. Mention the practical detail that a BNC-to-SMA adapter is needed to
connect the club Yagi, and that those adapters are cheap and easy to find.
Then say the important sentence clearly: this recommendation is conditional
because we have not had one in our hands and tested it yet. With the gear
picture in place, let's shift to the first place members can actually build

## Q&A Preparation

Use the answers below as short, steady responses. If a detail is still pending, say that directly rather than sounding more certain than the current plan allows.

- **Q:** Do I need a ham license to participate?
  **A:** For the Field Day demo, no; visitors can learn the hunt flow without
  prior experience. For club activities, any license class can
  participate, and complete beginners can still observe, pair up with a
  mentor, or use club guidance while they learn.

- **Q:** What gear do I need to get started?
  **A:** A basic 2-meter HT and a directional antenna are enough to begin. The
  club can demonstrate with its own foxes, and Radio Camp is meant to
  help people build the tape-measure Yagi and possibly an attenuator.

- **Q:** Why are both foxes on 147.42 MHz?
  **A:** Both current Byonics foxes are programmed the same way for simplicity:
  147.42 MHz simplex, K7LED ID, 15 seconds on and 15 seconds off. For
  now the plan is to deploy one at a time, with WWARA coordination still
  pending.

- **Q:** Is the RT-910B the club's official recommended radio?
  **A:** No, it is only a conditional recommendation right now. The appeal is the
  low price and visible S-meter, but it has not yet been hands-on
  tested, so say that clearly if members ask.

- **Q:** Will people be reimbursed for build materials?
  **A:** Do not promise reimbursement tonight. Say that partial reimbursement
  will be discussed with the board once actual costs are known.

- **Q:** Are the hunt dates final?
  **A:** No. July 19, August 22, and September 20 are the current target dates,
  but all three are tentative and will be confirmed closer to the
  events.

- **Q:** Can I borrow or loan gear?
  **A:** Yes to both, within limits. The Field Day demo especially benefits from
  loaner HTs and Yagis, so collect names from members willing to lend
  equipment or help coordinate check-in and return.

- **Q:** What if the Radio Camp build session runs short on time?
  **A:** The required project is the 2-meter tape-measure Yagi, so that gets
  priority. The step attenuator is second if time allows, and the dual-
  band Yagi is optional and intended for advanced builders.

- **Q:** What are the main safety concerns?
  **A:** For on-foot hunts, emphasize normal outdoor awareness and staying with
  the event plan. For the September mobile hunt, add the very specific
  reminder to stay clear of the BNSF tracks and bridge near the Howarth
  finish area.

- **Q:** How do I join the mailing list?
  **A:** Direct them to groups.io/g/mikeandkey-foxhunt and note that Scott KC7SAG
  is helping set it up. If the list or QR code is not live yet, collect
  names tonight and promise a follow-up once the list is ready.

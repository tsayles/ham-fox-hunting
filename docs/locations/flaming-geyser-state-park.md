## 2. Why Flaming Geyser

- ~480–503 acres in the Green River valley — woods, meadows, wetlands,
  and 3+ miles of river shoreline. Strong, varied RDF terrain.
- ~4.3 miles of hiking trails (Main, River, Wetland Loop, Whispering
  Firs Loop, Old Railroad Grade, Gorge Overlook, and more).
- Continuous forest and valley terrain — a good hunting challenge, and
  quiet on summer weekends.
- The river valley can introduce signal reflection (multipath): a
  realistic challenge for intermediate hounds; can frustrate first
  timers — brief accordingly.

> Transit access is effectively none; this plan assumes participants
> drive / carpool (transit requirement waived for this location).

## 3. Park rules & permit requirements

Source: Washington State Parks (parks.wa.gov), 2026.

### Do we need a permit or reservation?

- **Discover Pass required to park:** $10/day or $45/year, **per
  vehicle**. Coordinate passes for every carload (some annual free days
  exist, but mid-July is not one).
- **Special Activity Permit required for groups over 20 people.** A
  modest club turnout can trip it, even with a shelter reservation.
  Apply via the park office.
- **Shelters:** 4 reservable shelters (up to 50 each) via
  washington.goingtocamp.com or (888) CAMPOUT / (888) 226-7688; 200+
  first-come picnic tables.

### Operating rules to plan around

- **Hours:** open 8:00 AM – dusk, year-round. Our window is fine.
- **Day-use only** — no overnight camping.
- **Alcohol prohibited** throughout the park.
- **Dogs must be leashed** at all times.
- **Fires** only in designated pits/grills; check for summer fire bans.
- Popular summer **tubing / rafting** spot — river and picnic lots fill
  early on warm weekends; the trail network stays quieter.

## 5. Beacon (fox) placement

**Candidate:** Wooded trail area away from the river-tubing zone — e.g.,
the Whispering Firs Loop / Old Railroad Grade / Gorge Overlook side.

- Dense canopy and valley terrain for a genuine RDF challenge.
- Walkable on-trail; ~0.5–0.8 mi from the start for meaningful bearings.
- Keep within park boundaries and on public trails.

**Avoid:**

- The active river / swimming / tubing put-in areas (crowds, water
  safety).
- The model airplane flying area (potential RF noise).
- Private land adjacent to park boundaries.

**Geometry / safety notes:**

- Valley walls can reflect signals — expect multipath; pick a hide that
  still yields a usable bearing from the start.
- **Cell coverage may be spotty** — set a hard recall time, a meet-back
  point, and consider simplex radio check-ins for safety.

> NOTE: All start/beacon locations are planning candidates only.
> Confirm access, tree cover, and current conditions during a site walk
> before the event.
